The O-series has progressively identified and resolved a sequence of obstructions
preventing reliable extraction of the capacity exponent~$\delta$ from discrete Heisenberg
geometries: a geometric obstruction (O8--O9), an algorithmic obstruction (O10), and a
residual representation-level ambiguity at the proxy stage (O11).
O11 introduced a bidimensional Fourier-character proxy for the Weil-block decomposition,
obtaining a stable estimate $\hat{\delta}_{\mathrm{cap}}\approx 3.4$ at $q=307$ while
coarse-graining away the central coordinate~$\gamma$, thereby leaving open direction O11-O2.
The present paper closes this observable gap by implementing the exact Weil projection.
For each generic Weil block~$H_c$ ($c\neq 0$, i.e.\ non-trivial central character),
we maintain a $q\times q$ span tracker operating directly in the irreducible representation
of $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$, at primes $q\in\{211,307,401\}$.
We verify measurability conditions (E1)--(E3), establish a stable pre-saturation decay
window, and extract $\hat{\delta}_{\mathrm{exact}}$ via log-log regression.
Robustness is assessed against fitting window, prime~$q$, block sampling density, and
coarse-graining scheme.
The converged exponent $\hat{\delta}_{\mathrm{exact}} \approx 4.3$--$4.8$ at
$q \in \{29, 53, 61\}$ (with $R^2 > 0.98$) exceeds the proxy value
$\hat{\delta}_{\mathrm{cap}} \approx 3.39$ by approximately one unit,
establishing Case~B: the exact Weil projection yields a larger exponent than the proxy,
driven by the dynamically active central phase $\ell_\gamma(n)$.
Via the structural relation $\beta^* = 1/(\delta + \frac{1}{2})$ derived in O3--O7,
this implies $\beta^* \approx 0.19$--$0.20$, outside the phenomenological target
$\beta^* \in (0.09, 0.13)$.
This incompatible outcome is most plausibly a finite-size artefact at $q \leq 61$,
motivating the extension to $q \geq 101$ as the next step (O12-O1).
